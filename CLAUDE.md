# Gunsmith3D: read this first

## The vision

Gunsmith3D is a firearm design sandbox, with a career mode to come later. The designer is a **blank workshop, not a catalogue of gun types**.

- **Anything a player can imagine should be buildable**, however impractical or "cursed": a shotgun-shell pistol, a bolt-action machine gun, a revolver with three barrels. If it can be drawn, it should be possible.
- **The game never decides what a gun "is".** The only classification is **one-handed or two-handed**. How a gun feeds, locks, fires and handles comes entirely from the parts the player made.
- **Usefulness is the player's problem.** Bad designs are allowed. They just perform badly: they kick hard, jam, or are unwieldy. Never block a design for being impractical.
- **Examples are inspiration, not templates.** The example guns (blowback pistol, tilting barrel, bolt action...) show what's possible and should be polished. But nothing in the code may be special-cased around them, and no feature should assume they're the kinds of firearm that exist.

An earlier version of this project was built as if the examples were the only kinds of gun. That was a misunderstanding of the intent. Don't repeat it.

## Design principles

- **Simulate physical primitives, never gun types.** Mechanisms should emerge from parts, joints, springs, contacts, pressure and cartridges, not from code for "a revolver" or "a pump action".
- **What players must design vs. what can be abstracted** (the owner's rule):
  - Always real, designed parts: **feeding, the bolt and breech, and extraction**.
  - Optional abstraction, with an advanced drawn alternative: **fire control** (trigger, hammer or striker, sear, disconnector). Built-in fire control still has to be cocked by the action.
  - Fine detail that's tiny, hidden or fiddly to draw (feed lips, magazine springs) becomes **settings with good defaults that have real consequences** in the physics. Don't add a setting that does nothing: measure its effect first, and if it has none, leave it out and say so.
- **Unreliable designs jam for real.** On the bench through the physics, and later in live fire by rolling against the gun's measured jam rate. Clearing a jam means running the action.

## Known assumptions still to remove

These are what currently lock the designer. They are the roadmap, roughly in this order:

1. **Several chambers.** Done: `BreechDef::more` (further chambers, each with its own breech part, chamber part, face and muzzle) and `fireOrder` (turn or together). In the simulation, chamber 0's state lives in Sim's usual fields and `useChamber(S, k)` swaps another in (`Sim::chambers`); rounds carry `slot`. The over-under derringer example (hinged, tips up). Still to do: revolver cylinders and pepperboxes (chambers that index round to one barrel: parts turning about **any axis the player places**, not only the bore; out of the side-view plane, so a simplified treatment), a magazine feeding a chamber other than the first, and a drawn striker per chamber.
2. **Kinds of ammunition.** Done: `Cartridge::kind` (centerfire, rimfire, shotshell, caseless), `projectiles`, pellets (`shotDia`), brass head and hull colour; the pump shotgun example. Rimfire only matters to a drawn striker (it must hit the rim); caseless leaves no case. Still to do: any chamber cut for its own cartridge (comes with item 1), and pellets as separate projectiles once the firing range exists.
3. **Gas.** Done: `BreechDef::gas` (`GasPortDef`: bore, `at` mm from the face, port and piston diameters, stroke, push angle; JSON `gas`). `gasForces` (solver.cpp) each substep: the bullet accelerates evenly, passes the port, bore pressure behind it falls along the bore (2.3x average at the breech, a third at the muzzle) and blows down 0.3 ms after exit; gas flows through the port into a cylinder that grows as the piston moves (flow constant 60, tuned so AR-15/AK carriers leave at about 6 m/s; the bore is drawn down by the cylinder's volume); the push stops once the piston has moved its stroke. Force at the piston's centre, reaction on the chamber part. A spring on the piston's assembly counts as `breechSprung`. Open bolt: `BreechDef::openBolt` (built-in fire control): `searHeld` holds the breech back at `searPoint` (cockAt, or round length + 6 mm, or feed + 2 with simplified feeding) by a position solve before the joints; the trigger frees it (`boltFree`), it fires when it closes on a round; `searUp` catches it on the way back. It fires only when home: in battery and either within 0.15 mm of the face or no longer closing (relative speed < 0.05 m/s), not merely inside `inBattery`'s 1 mm. Drawn fixed firing pins: a striker in the breech's assembly is timed against the chamber. Still to do: a gas example gun (the pump's action is built for hand speed and doesn't eject at gas speed), gas on the range's felt recoil beyond the sim.
4. **Feed devices as building blocks.** Done so far: any part can be a removable or built-in magazine, with a drawn round path (side by side or nose to tail) and a shaped insertion path (bends, rock-in tilt); tubes feed physically (the spring pushes each round out of the mouth as a real round, onto whatever the player drew: see the pump-action example, with its lifter, action lock and lost motion). Belts: `BodyDef::feedPawl`/`pawlStroke`, the pawl catches the next link at the end of its stroke and pushes the belt on its way back, only into an empty tray (belt-fed blowback example; the belt itself is drawn across the gun, out of plane). Clips: `clip` stripper/enbloc (`fillMagazines`, `Ev::ClipOut`); the clip itself isn't a physical part yet. Magazines at any angle and on top: the presented round carries `liftL` (the follower's push, separate from the feed direction `feedL`, which always points at the muzzle), and a feed ramp tips the nose toward the bore either way. Side magazines (`MagGen::side`, simplified): in the side view only a mouth box at the feed spot, the body across the gun (`z`, `thick` = stack length); the presented round has `sideFeed`, and whatever rides over it pushes it in (`pushedIn`, drawn with `zVis`) instead of down; `solveSideWell` slides the magazine in and out along z (`Joint::zOut`). `BreechDef::extractor` top/bottom/auto (auto: away from the magazine). Editing: `MagGen::extraH/extraW` (a fitted magazine longer/wider than it needs), `byHand` (reshaped: `generateMagazine` leaves the outline; set by `freezeMagazine`, which first lays a `roundPath` from `autoRoundPath` so the presented round doesn't move); `MagGen::pts0` (not saved) is the outline as fitted, and `commit()` freezes a fitted magazine whose outline no longer matches; `moveBody`/`rotateBody` carry `gen.top`/`angle`/`pts0` along. A selected magazine shows its round path's dots (automatic ones too: dragging one makes the path explicit), and its well's seat (`Seated here`: dragging it moves the magazine). Still to do: revolver cylinders (with item 1), and several feed devices per gun (the simulation uses the first latched or built-in magazine).
5. **How it's held.** Done: `Design::pose` (`HandHold` firing, support, butt on parts; `BodyHold` aim, hip, lowered: eye in gun coordinates, body yaw, muzzle tilt; `holdOf()` fills unposed holds), `heldWith()`. Edited in the bench's Pose mode (`app/pose.cpp`: upper-body mannequin built from lofted meshes (`loft`, `shapes()`: shirted torso, head with ear defenders, tapered arms), two-bone IK arms that go red when short; a red line of sight from the aim eye along the bore; `BodyHold::pocket` is the shoulder pocket per hold (body frame from the eye, JSON `pocket` only when moved), dragged by its dot or set by `poseBedShoulder`). The range's ADS uses the posed aim eye when the Aim hold is set. Hands: `core/hand.cpp` is the hand skeleton (19 named bones with start, end and up, from `DigitPose` bends and spread per digit; presets; the thumb's first bone is its metacarpal, rooted by the wrist, turned so it curls across the palm; `DigitPose::oppose` swings it round the palm's long axis; `digitLimits`/`clampDigit` real joint ranges; `reachDigit` damped-least-squares reach, used by fingertip drags in `app/pose.cpp`, which slide the target along the mouse ray to stay within reach): the framework for a modelled, skinned hand later. The owner chose: the pose replaces the designer's grip markers (old `grips` load into the pose), three holds posed separately, an upper-body mannequin. Animations (`Design::anims`, `AnimKey`: time, both `HandHold`s, `gripF/gripS`, `event` dropmag/freshmag; `core/anim.cpp` `animAt` curves through the keys from and back to the hold): slots inspect, reload, reload_empty, clear_jam, control:<id>. In the sim `playAnim` / `animTick` (every substep, in `run`) drive `Sim::animHand[2]`, the same grip as the mouse's hand. Edited in Pose mode (`viewPose`, `editHand` make a key at the timeline's moment). R and control keys play them on the bench; empty slots do the plain action. The owner chose keyframes moved through smoothly, and animations that follow the gun (`animTick` holds the clock while a gripped part is still coming, up to 1 s). `AnimKey::turn` turns a gripped part that rolls (via `Sim::rollHand`). `AnimKey::gun[6]` moves the whole gun (forward, up, right mm; muzzle up, turn, roll degrees) about the firing hand, curved through the keys like the hands (`AnimHands::gun`, `gunMoved`); applied by `poseGunMatrix` (via `gunMoveMatrix`) in Pose and to `R.G` on the range, not on the bench. The panel's Timeline length is editor-only. Inspect (I, `Act::Inspect`): the range plays "inspect" or `defaultInspect` (gun keys only). Work the action (slot work_action, key B): `autoWorkAction` (reliability.cpp) makes keys from the same analysis the reliability Runner uses (breech assembly or "handle" part, stroke, rolling, hinged), at about 1 m/s. Next: first-person shooting using the pose and these animations, Tarkov-like weighty handling, holstering.
6. **Rebuild and polish the examples** on these blocks, with no special code. Feeding has been made robust: the bolt action's ramp is longer (all bullet shapes feed when worked by hand), and the built-in cartridges now have real shapes. Still open: on the tilting-barrel pistol, racking out a live round is a knife-edge (26 of 30 shape and nose-length combinations). Measured cause: the rim meets the ejector only at the very end of the slide's travel, so a long live round is tipped about 14 degrees (it needs 18 to be let go, `cartridges.cpp`) before the slide bounces off its stop and pushes it back in. Tried and rejected: letting rounds go at the same front-end offset as a case (12/30: released low, they miss the port) and moving the ejector forward (it catches the feeding round: no feeding at all). The real fix is reworking that example's slide travel and port, not the physics.

Barrels (`BodyDef::barrel`, `BarrelDef`: cartridge, rifled, twist, grooves, muzzleEnd; the Barrel tool, `makeBarrel` in `app/editor.cpp`): `syncBarrels()` (core) sets the breech's chamber, face and muzzle from barrel parts (first barrel = chamber 0, others = `more` with `ownCart`); called on every commit and when the simulation is built. Designs without barrels keep the placed face and muzzle. Per-chamber cartridges are moved in and out of `design.breech.cart` / `chamberR` / `impulse` by `useChamber`. Bore and chamber are display-only cuts (render.cpp), skipped while a part is being edited (`BodyMesh::rough`, rebuilt 0.6 s after the last change); `bodyBias` (the anti-flicker depth nudge) is 0 for round parts and at most 0.12 mm for the rest (it used to grow with the part index and shifted late barrels off their bore); rifling is drawn as helical lines (`drawRifling`). `spinStability` (Miller) rates the twist; the range spreads and slows bullets that are marginal or tumble. Barrel axes are centred on the outline (`roundAxis`). Tilt: `BodyDef::tiltX/tiltY`, `carve::tiltSection` slices the tilted solid for the physics; the mesh is turned in render.cpp.

Round (turned) parts (`src/core/round.cpp`, `latheMesh` in `app/gfx.cpp`): the outline spun about an axis, with sides, turn and sweep; the physics uses the same depth slabs as carving, each the round shape's outline at that depth.

Carving (`src/core/carve.cpp`, `csg.cpp`, `app/carvetool.cpp`): parts can be cut in 3D from any direction. Snapping (`snapCarve`): the entry point snaps along each design axis lying in the face (|direction| < 0.15) to the part's middle, other cuts, outline corners (within ~7 px), else the grid step; Alt is free; guides drawn by `drawGuides`. A selected part's cut dots can be dragged across their face plane (`A.carveMove`, applied on release). The display cuts the real solid (own BSP code; adding an outside geometry library was blocked). The physics slices a carved part through its depth into up to 16 welded slabs, each the outline minus the cuts there. Not yet in the physics: cuts on magazines and on parts that turn on the bore.

Scale tool (`app/scaletool.cpp`, `Tool::Scale`): `scaleParts` scales a welded piece (or picked corners) about a point in x, y and thickness, with everything attached at points on it (as `moveBody`/`rotateBody` do), carves, marks, depth shaping, mass you set; a fitted magazine is frozen first. Handles: a box round the piece, pick then drag (corner: proportional, Shift free, Ctrl about the middle; edges: one axis); live from a copy of the design taken at the press.

Combining outlines (`src/core/outline.cpp`): `combineOutlines` (merge, keep overlap, cut away) works on a fine grid, then snaps the traced result onto the true corners, edge crossings and edges, so it is exact for straight-edged and rounded-corner parts (rounding is handed back where corners survive); `chamferCorners`. Pieces (`app/pieces.cpp`): a part, its welded piece and their joints as JSON with a `gunsmithPiece` marker, for Ctrl+C/V and the parts library in `user/parts`.

Live mirrors (`src/core/mirror.cpp`): `BodyDef::mirror` tag pairs two parts, `mirrorKind` depth (across the gun) or line (`mirrorA/B`); `syncMirrors` copies shape and look from the edited one (in `commit()` by diffing against the last snapshot, and every frame from the selected part). Faces tool (`app/faces.cpp`): picks the near/far face or a side wall of any untilted, unturned part; drag pushes it along its normal, E extrudes (side wall: two new corners; near/far face: a new welded piece). In the Select tool every face of the selected part works Sprocket-style (`selectFacesInput`): the face under the mouse is washed in yellow, a click picks it (`facePicked`; `pressFace` remembers the face under a press, and a press on any face of the selected part counts as a press on the part), a press on the picked face pushes it (`facePushStart`, Shift extrudes), a drag elsewhere moves the part, Alt-click picks the part behind. The far face's corners (circles) drag as in the Faces tool. An extruded side wall leaves its new face picked and its two corners selected (to push further or taper); every drawn corner gets a depth line, so the seam shows. The Faces tool's first click picks the part and the face together. Corner handles sit on the face toward the camera (`bodyFront`). Corner limit 1024 (`MAX_CORNERS`): `Body::acc` in the solver (grid of edges within contact reach, plus a tight 0.5 mm list, and horizontal bands for inside tests) keeps contacts exact and fast; parts drawn with more than 32 corners get thinned contact samples. Shaped in depth (`src/core/depth.cpp`): `BodyDef::depthOff/depthZn/depthZf` (JSON `depth`) give each corner a near point and a far point; `depthRef` lets them follow corner edits (`normalizeDepth` in `commit()`). `depthSection` is the exact cut at a depth (the physics slabs, `carve::slices`), `depthTriangles` the display mesh. Per-corner thickness in the Shape section (*Thickness here* / *Across* for the picked corners) sets the same depthZn/depthZf. Edited in the Faces tool (corner squares/circles; dragged in the axis plane the view faces most, `App::cornerPlane`, chosen at press; Z for depth). Not for tilted or round parts; corners sharp.

Engravings (`src/core/marks.cpp`, `app/engrave.cpp`): `BodyDef::marks` (`MarkDef`: points on the surface in design mm with normals, or text at a point with a reading direction; width, colour; JSON `marks`), looks only. `strokeText` is a single-stroke lettering (4x6 grid glyphs). `followMarks` (every frame in the designer, and in `commit()`) carries them along when the outline moves as a rigid whole (best-fit turn, with or without a flip) and keeps them on the near/far face when the part moves in depth or changes thickness; reshaping leaves them in place. Drawn as thin strips in each part's draw transform (`drawMarks`), so they ride along on the bench. Built-in gun finishes (`assets/textures`, the owner's; embedded by `tools/embed_textures.py` into `app/builtin_textures.inc`): textures named `builtin:<id>`.

Hard limit to keep in mind: the physics works in the side-view plane plus depth. Shapes can now be fully 3D (carving), but parts still only move in that plane. Things that move *across* the gun (swing-out cylinders, side-mounted magazines, cross-bolt safeties) need a simplified treatment. Do these last, and be upfront about it.

Firing range (`app/range.cpp`, `Screen::Range`): the picked gun replaces `A.design` (stashed like the bench) and runs as the bench sim (`A.S`); the map's player moves as in map walking. The whole gun scene (`drawGunOnly`, the designer's gun drawing without the bench) is drawn under an rlgl matrix `R.G` that maps the gun's drawing frame to the eye (aim: the pose's aim eye, raised over the top of the gun, `sightY`; ready: lower and to the right), with lag, sway and kick. `Ev::Bang` fires each projectile from the muzzle along the bore (point mass, gravity, quadratic drag), with a zero angle (25 m one hand, 100 m shoulder). `target_paper` map entities (in `builtinRange`) score rings. Live-fire jams come from the sim itself. Not yet: first-person hands, walls clipping the gun, cases in the world.

## Technical notes

- C++17, CMake. Layers: `src/core` (physics, designs, cartridges, reliability, maps; no graphics) → `src/app` (raylib rendering, editor, bench) → `src/ui` (Dear ImGui panels) → `src/main.cpp`. See README "Source layout".
- Headless core and tests: `cmake -S . -B build && cmake --build build -j && ./build/gunsmith3d_tests`
- Native Linux app for screenshots (developer only): `-DGUNSMITH_NATIVE_APP=ON`, then run under `xvfb-run` with `LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE=1`. `--frames N --shot file.png` takes a screenshot (saved in the working directory). See `src/main.cpp` for `--preset`, `--select`, `--test`, `--fire`, `--side`, `--tutorial N`, `--mapedit`, and the `GUNSMITH_TAB` variable.
- Windows build from Linux: MinGW-w64 (`g++-mingw-w64-x86-64-posix` from apt) with a toolchain file setting `CMAKE_SYSTEM_NAME Windows` and the `x86_64-w64-mingw32-*-posix` compilers. The project's `cmake-mingw-toolchain.cmake` expects llvm-mingw instead.
- Example guns live in `src/core/presets.inc`, generated by `tools/make_presets.py`. Edit the script, not the .inc.
- Player files (`app/paths.cpp`): the app folder is Documents/OAB Foundry/Gunsmith (`initAppFolder`: settings.json, autosave.json, Crashes; falls back to the game folder), the user folder defaults to its `User` (`defaultUserFolder`, or `Settings::userDir` from Settings > Files, `changeUserFolder` copies files over) with Blueprints, Levels, Textures, Parts, NPCs, Rounds (one JSON per cartridge, `setUserCartridgesDir`), Groups, Characters/character.json. The path globals (`BLUEPRINTS_DIR`, `MAPS_DIR`...) are re-pointed by `setUserFolder`; `setupUserFolders` moves files from the old `user/...`, `blueprints/`, `maps/` folders. Animations stay inside blueprints. Textures: `app/usertex.cpp`; the Windows file box and folder picker are in `app/sysfiles.cpp` (kept apart from raylib because of windows.h name clashes).
